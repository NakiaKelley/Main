## 1. PRODUCT OVERVIEW

### 1.1 Product Description
The BBQ Chicken Wrap is a cold-assembled boxed lunch featuring house-marinated, oven-smoked pulled chicken (mix of breasts and thighs), Kansas City BBQ sauce, shredded pepperjack cheese, creamy coleslaw, and bread & butter pickle chips, all wrapped in a 12" garlic herb tortilla. This Southern-inspired wrap showcases BBQ flavor development through overnight marinating and oven-smoking technique, delivering tender pulled chicken with authentic smokehouse taste.

### 1.2 Mission Alignment
**Workforce Development Focus:**
- BBQ marinade preparation and flavor understanding
- Oven-smoking technique using liquid smoke (accessible alternative to traditional smoking)
- Low-and-slow cooking method for tender chicken
- Chicken pulling/shredding technique
- Food safety with raw poultry (cross-contamination prevention, proper cooking)
- Multi-day production planning
- Assembly with multiple textures and flavors
- Portion control with saucy components

**Food Rescue Integration:**
- Opportunistic utilization of rescued produce when available and meeting quality standards
- Coleslaw can incorporate rescued cabbage and carrots
- Flexibility in ingredient sourcing while maintaining product consistency

**Sustainability Considerations:**
- Chicken trim utilization in other menu items
- Reusable containers for marinating (reduce plastic waste)
- Composting of vegetable scraps
- Recyclable/compostable packaging options
- Mix of chicken breasts and thighs reduces waste (utilizes different cuts)

### 1.3 Product Specifications
- **Yield per Batch:** 6 wraps (approximately 14-16 oz each)
- **Shelf Life:** 3-5 days refrigerated at 38°F-40°F
- **Assembly Method:** Cold assembly only
- **Production Timeline:** 2-day process (Day 1: marinade prep, Day 2: oven-smoking and assembly)
- **Packaging:** Deli paper wrap + craft box or clamshell
- **Delivery Schedule:** Monday PM and Friday AM wholesale deliveries

### 1.4 Unique Features
- House-marinated BBQ chicken with overnight flavor development
- Oven-smoked using liquid smoke (accessible, consistent smokehouse flavor)
- Pulled chicken texture (tender, authentic BBQ experience)
- Mix of chicken breasts and thighs (flavor and texture variety)
- Sweet and tangy flavor profile with heat from pepperjack
- Creamy coleslaw and pickles provide cooling contrast
- Garlic herb wrap complements BBQ flavors

### 2.2 Ingredient Quality Standards

**Chicken (Breasts and Thighs):**
- Fresh, not frozen (or fully thawed if frozen)
- No discoloration or off-odors
- Breasts: Lean, uniform size
- Thighs: More fat and connective tissue (adds flavor and moisture)
- Store at 38°F-40°F until marinating
- Use within manufacturer's date guidelines
- USDA inspected

**BBQ Marinade Ingredients:**
- Ketchup: Standard tomato ketchup (Heinz or comparable)
- Apple cider vinegar: Not white vinegar (different flavor)
- Brown sugar: Dark brown adds more molasses flavor; light brown is milder
- Worcestershire sauce: Essential for umami depth (contains anchovies - note for allergens)
- Liquid smoke: Hickory or mesquite flavor acceptable; start with less if unsure of strength
- Spices: Fresh spices have more potent flavor
- Dijon mustard: Acts as emulsifier

**Kansas City BBQ Sauce:**
- Thick, sweet, tomato-based style
- High quality commercial brand (Sweet Baby Ray's, KC Masterpiece, Gates)
- Consistency: Thick and clingy (coats chicken well)
- Flavor: Sweet with tangy undertones, mild spice
- Store at room temperature unopened; refrigerate after opening

**Pepperjack Cheese:**
- Shredded (not sliced)
- Contains visible jalapeño pieces
- Fresh (not dried out or clumped)
- Store refrigerated

**Coleslaw:**
- Creamy, mayo-based (not vinegar slaw)
- Store-bought for efficiency
- Should be crunchy, not soggy
- Check expiration date
- Keep refrigerated

**Bread & Butter Pickles:**
- Sweet pickle style (not dill)
- Chips (not spears or whole)
- Crisp texture
- Not excessively wet or soggy
- Store refrigerated after opening

**Garlic Herb Wraps:**
- Fresh (not expired)
- Pliable, not dry or cracking
- Visible garlic and herb seasoning
- Even color
- No tears or holes
- Store in original packaging until use

## 5. PRODUCTION PROCEDURES - DAY 1: BBQ MARINADE PREPARATION

### 5.1 BBQ Marinade Preparation

**Time Required:** 15 minutes  
**Skill Level:** Basic  
**Training Focus:** Marinade mixing, flavor balance, understanding BBQ components

**Procedure:**

1. **Gather BBQ Marinade Ingredients**
   - Ketchup: ½ cup (4 oz)
   - Apple cider vinegar: ¼ cup (2 oz)
   - Brown sugar: ¼ cup packed (60g)
   - Worcestershire sauce: 2 Tbsp (1 oz)
   - Liquid smoke: 1 Tbsp (15ml)
   - Garlic powder: 1 Tbsp
   - Onion powder: 1 Tbsp
   - Smoked paprika: 1 Tbsp
   - Black pepper: 1 tsp
   - Salt: 1½ tsp
   - Cayenne pepper (optional): ½ tsp
   - Dijon mustard: 1 Tbsp

2. **Combine Ingredients in Mixing Bowl**
   - Place all ingredients in large mixing bowl
   - Start with ketchup as base
   - Add liquids (vinegar, Worcestershire, liquid smoke)
   - Add brown sugar
   - Add all spices and mustard

3. **Whisk Thoroughly**
   - Whisk vigorously for 1-2 minutes
   - Ensure brown sugar fully dissolved (no grittiness)
   - Marinade should be smooth and well-combined
   - Color should be deep reddish-brown
   - Consistency should be thick but pourable

4. **Taste and Adjust**
   - Marinade should taste: sweet (brown sugar), tangy (vinegar), smoky (liquid smoke), savory (spices)
   - **Note:** Marinade will taste strong on its own (this is correct - flavors mellow during marinating and cooking)
   - If too sweet: Add 1 Tbsp more vinegar
   - If too tangy: Add 1 Tbsp more brown sugar or ketchup
   - If not smoky enough: Add ½ tsp more liquid smoke (be cautious - easy to overdo)
   - If too spicy: Omit or reduce cayenne pepper
   - Document any adjustments on batch log

5. **Store Temporarily**
   - Can use marinade immediately or prepare a few hours ahead
   - Keep at room temperature until ready to add chicken (easier mixing)
   - Do not store overnight without chicken (food safety)

**Quality Standards:**
- Well-combined (no separated ingredients)
- Brown sugar fully dissolved
- Thick consistency (coats spoon)
- Deep reddish-brown color
- Balanced sweet-tangy-smoky flavor

**Training Notes:**
- Explain purpose of each ingredient:
  - Ketchup: Base, sweetness, acidity, tomato flavor
  - Vinegar: Tenderizes chicken, adds tang
  - Brown sugar: Sweetness, caramelization during cooking
  - Worcestershire: Umami depth, complexity
  - Liquid smoke: Authentic smokehouse flavor without smoker
  - Spices: Seasoning and flavor development
  - Mustard: Emulsifier, helps flavors bind
- Discuss how liquid smoke works (condensed smoke particles)
- Teach importance of tasting and adjusting
- Emphasize that strong marinade flavor is intentional

**Yield:** Approximately 1¼ cups (10 oz) marinade - sufficient for 1.5 lbs chicken

### 5.2 Chicken Preparation and Marinating

**Time Required:** 10 minutes prep + overnight marinating  
**Skill Level:** Basic  
**Training Focus:** Raw chicken handling, food safety, proper marinating technique

**Procedure:**

1. **Prepare Raw Chicken Work Area**
   - Use designated raw chicken cutting board
   - Don gloves
   - Have zip-top bags or marinating container ready
   - Keep chicken refrigerated until ready to prep

2. **Inspect Chicken**
   - Remove chicken breasts and thighs from packaging
   - Inspect for quality: no discoloration, off-odors, or sliminess
   - Discard any chicken that appears compromised
   - Note: Chicken thighs may appear slightly darker (this is normal)

3. **Rinse and Pat Dry**
   - Rinse chicken pieces under cold running water
   - Pat dry thoroughly with paper towels
   - Drying helps marinade adhere better
   - Discard used paper towels immediately

4. **Trim if Needed**
   - Breasts: Remove any excess fat or connective tissue
   - Thighs: Can remove excess skin or large fat deposits if desired (some fat is good for flavor and moisture)
   - Trim to relatively uniform thickness for even cooking
   - Save trim for other uses (stock) or discard

5. **Place Chicken in Marinating Container**
   - **Option A: Gallon-size zip-top bag**
     - Place all chicken pieces (breasts and thighs together) in bag
     - Pour BBQ marinade over chicken
     - Seal bag, removing as much air as possible
     - Massage bag gently to coat chicken evenly
     - Lay flat in refrigerator for maximum surface contact
   
   - **Option B: Covered container with lid**
     - Place chicken in container
     - Pour marinade over chicken
     - Turn chicken pieces to coat all sides
     - Cover with tight-fitting lid

6. **Ensure Full Coverage**
   - All chicken surfaces should be in contact with marinade
   - Turn/flip chicken pieces to ensure even coating
   - Marinade should coat all pieces generously

7. **Label Container**
   - Label with: "Raw Chicken - BBQ Marinade - Date - Time - Use By [Next Day]"
   - Include time marinating started for tracking

8. **Refrigerate**
   - Place in refrigerator immediately
   - Store at 38°F-40°F
   - Place on bottom shelf or in designated raw protein area (prevents cross-contamination if leaks)
   - Marinating time: **Minimum 8 hours, maximum 24 hours**
   - **Ideal timing:** 12-16 hours for optimal flavor penetration

9. **Turn Halfway Through**
   - After 4-6 hours (or halfway through marinating time)
   - Remove from refrigerator briefly
   - Flip bag or turn chicken pieces in container
   - Return to refrigerator immediately
   - This ensures even marinade distribution

**Critical Control Points:**
- Keep raw chicken at ≤40°F at all times (except brief handling)
- Use separate cutting board and utensils for raw chicken
- Wash hands thoroughly after handling raw chicken
- Sanitize all surfaces that contacted raw chicken
- Do not cross-contaminate with other foods
- Label clearly as raw chicken

**Training Notes:**
- Teach proper raw chicken handling and cross-contamination prevention
- Explain how acid (vinegar) and salt in marinade tenderize chicken
- Discuss optimal marinating times (too short = less flavor; too long = mushy texture)
- Emphasize importance of temperature control
- Demonstrate proper handwashing technique after handling raw poultry
- Discuss difference between breasts (lean) and thighs (more fat/connective tissue)

**Marinating Science:**
- Acids (vinegar) partially denature proteins = tenderizing
- Sugar helps chicken brown and caramelize during cooking
- Salt enhances flavor and helps retain moisture
- Liquid smoke penetrates meat surface, adding smokehouse flavor
- Spices infuse aromatic flavor

**Food Safety Reminders:**
- Never reuse marinade that contacted raw chicken
- Never serve raw marinade as sauce (even if boiled - risk remains)
- Always cook chicken to 165°F regardless of marinating time
- Discard marinade after removing chicken (or use as basting liquid during cooking only)

## 6. PRODUCTION PROCEDURES - DAY 2: OVEN-SMOKING & ASSEMBLY

### 6.1 Oven-Smoking Chicken

**Time Required:** 90-120 minutes (15 min prep + 90-120 min cooking + cooling)  
**Skill Level:** Intermediate  
**Training Focus:** Low-and-slow cooking, temperature monitoring, achieving pull-apart tenderness

**Procedure:**

1. **Preheat Oven**
   - Set oven to **275°F** (low temperature for tender, fall-apart chicken)
   - Allow 15-20 minutes for thorough preheating
   - Verify temperature with oven thermometer if available

2. **Prepare Baking Dish**
   - Select oven-safe baking dish or roasting pan large enough to hold all chicken in single layer
   - Lightly spray or oil bottom of dish (prevents sticking)
   - Have lid or heavy-duty aluminum foil ready for covering

3. **Transfer Chicken to Baking Dish**
   - Remove marinated chicken from refrigerator
   - Place chicken pieces (breasts and thighs) in baking dish in single layer
   - **Do not overlap** - chicken should have space around each piece for even cooking
   - Pour all marinade over chicken (marinade becomes cooking liquid and intensifies flavor)
   - **Note:** Some operations prefer to discard marinade and just use residual coating on chicken - either method acceptable

4. **Cover Tightly**
   - If using lid: Place lid on baking dish, ensure tight seal
   - If using foil: Cover dish tightly with heavy-duty aluminum foil
   - **CRITICAL:** Tight seal is essential to trap moisture and smoke flavor
   - Foil should be crimped tightly around edges
   - Can use double layer of foil for extra seal

5. **Place in Preheated Oven**
   - Carefully place covered baking dish in center of oven
   - Set timer for 90 minutes (initial check)

6. **Monitor Cooking**
   - After 90 minutes, carefully remove dish from oven
   - **Be careful of hot steam** when uncovering
   - Check chicken temperature with food thermometer
   - Insert thermometer into thickest part of chicken pieces
   - Avoid touching bone (if any remain) or pan bottom

7. **Check for Doneness**
   - **Temperature requirement:** 165°F minimum in all pieces
   - **Tenderness test:** Chicken should be very tender, almost falling apart
   - Thighs typically take longer than breasts
   - If not at 165°F or not tender enough, re-cover and continue cooking
   - Check every 15 minutes until done

8. **Visual Indicators of Doneness**
   - Chicken should pull apart easily with fork
   - Meat should be completely opaque (no pink)
   - Breasts: White throughout
   - Thighs: Slightly darker but still fully cooked
   - Should see some caramelization on surface

9. **Remove from Oven**
   - Once all chicken reaches 165°F and is fall-apart tender, remove from oven
   - Let rest in dish for 10-15 minutes (covered)
   - Resting allows juices to redistribute

10. **Cool Chicken**
    - After resting, uncover chicken
    - Transfer chicken pieces to clean sheet pan (leave cooking liquid behind)
    - **Discard cooking liquid/marinade** (food safety - contacted raw chicken)
    - Spread chicken on sheet pan (not stacked)
    - Place sheet pan in walk-in refrigerator
    - **CRITICAL:** Cool to ≤40°F before pulling/shredding
    - Cooling time: 60-90 minutes
    - Stir or rearrange halfway through for even cooling
    - Verify temperature before proceeding

**Critical Control Points:**
- **CCP 1:** All chicken must reach 165°F internal temperature (kills harmful bacteria)
- **CCP 2:** Prevent cross-contamination (use clean tools for cooked chicken)
- **CCP 3:** Cool to ≤40°F before pulling (prevents bacterial growth during handling)

**Quality Indicators:**
- Internal temperature 165°F+ (definitive indicator)
- Very tender (should pull apart easily)
- Fully opaque (no pink color)
- Rich BBQ/smoky aroma
- Slight caramelization on surface

**Training Notes:**
- Teach proper thermometer use (thickest part, avoid pan)
- Explain "low and slow" cooking method (tender, moist results)
- Discuss how covering traps moisture and smoke flavor
- Practice identifying visual doneness cues
- Emphasize patience - rushing leads to tough chicken
- Explain difference in cooking time for breasts vs. thighs

**Common Mistakes to Avoid:**
- Cooking at too high temperature (leads to dry, tough chicken)
- Not covering tightly (moisture and smoke escape)
- Undercooking (food safety risk)
- Not allowing chicken to cool before pulling (food safety risk, also harder to handle)

**Oven-Smoking Tips:**
- Liquid smoke provides authentic smokehouse flavor without equipment
- Low temperature (275°F) breaks down connective tissue = tender
- Covering creates steam environment = moist chicken
- Longer cooking time = more tender (within reason)
- Mix of breasts and thighs = varied texture and flavor

### 6.2 Pulling/Shredding Chicken

**Time Required:** 20 minutes  
**Skill Level:** Basic to Intermediate  
**Training Focus:** Shredding technique, texture consistency, portion control

**Procedure:**

1. **Prepare Work Area**
   - Use dedicated cutting board or work directly from bowl
   - Ensure chicken fully cooled to ≤40°F (verify with thermometer)
   - Have two forks ready (sturdy forks work best)
   - Have mixing bowl ready for pulled chicken
   - Don gloves

2. **Inspect Cooled Chicken**
   - Check for proper color (cooked chicken is opaque)
   - No pink color should remain
   - Check for any off-odors (properly cooked chicken smells savory)
   - Chicken should be very tender

3. **Begin Pulling Process**
   - Take one piece of chicken at a time
   - Using two forks, insert tines into chicken
   - Pull forks in opposite directions
   - Chicken should shred easily along natural grain
   - If chicken doesn't shred easily, it may need more cooking (assess and cook longer if needed)

4. **Shredding Technique**
   - Pull chicken into bite-sized pieces
   - Target size: roughly ½ to 1 inch pieces
   - Not too fine (loses texture), not too chunky (hard to eat in wrap)
   - Follow natural grain of meat for best texture
   - Breasts: Will shred into longer, finer strands
   - Thighs: Will shred into slightly chunkier, more irregular pieces

5. **Remove Unwanted Bits**
   - As you pull chicken, remove:
     - Any remaining fat deposits (small amounts okay)
     - Gristle or tough connective tissue
     - Any bones (if boneless chicken had small fragments)
   - Discard these bits

6. **Mix Breasts and Thighs Together**
   - Combine pulled breast meat and thigh meat in bowl
   - Mix together for variety of texture and flavor
   - Breasts provide lean protein
   - Thighs provide moisture and rich flavor

7. **Assess Texture**
   - Pulled chicken should look: shredded, not ground or diced
   - Moist appearance (not dry)
   - Mix of fine strands and small chunks
   - Should look similar to traditional pulled pork

8. **Portion for Later Use**
   - Total yield: approximately 18-20 oz pulled chicken (from 24 oz raw)
   - Will need 24 oz total for 6 wraps (4 oz each)
   - Note: Some moisture lost during cooking, but chicken absorbs BBQ sauce later

9. **Store Temporarily**
   - Place pulled chicken in covered container
   - Do not add BBQ sauce yet (will do in next step)
   - Keep refrigerated at 38°F-40°F
   - Use immediately or within 2 hours

**Critical Control Point:**
- Keep chicken at ≤40°F throughout pulling process
- Minimize time at room temperature

**Training Notes:**
- Demonstrate proper fork technique (pulling, not mashing)
- Teach how to identify grain direction
- Practice consistent shredding (not too fine, not too chunky)
- Discuss difference between breast meat (lean, light) and thigh meat (rich, dark)
- Show how to remove unwanted bits efficiently
- Emphasize food safety (gloves, cold temperature)

**Quality Standards:**
- Shredded into bite-sized pieces (½-1 inch)
- Mix of fine strands and small chunks
- Moist appearance (not dry)
- No large fat deposits or gristle
- Even texture throughout

**Yield:** Approximately 18-20 oz pulled chicken (before adding BBQ sauce)

### 6.3 Mixing Pulled Chicken with BBQ Sauce

**Time Required:** 5 minutes  
**Skill Level:** Basic  
**Training Focus:** Sauce coating technique, flavor balance, portion management

**Procedure:**

1. **Prepare Work Area**
   - Have medium mixing bowl ready
   - Pulled chicken ready
   - Kansas City BBQ sauce measured and ready
   - Spoon or gloved hands for mixing

2. **Add Pulled Chicken to Bowl**
   - Place all pulled chicken (18-20 oz) in mixing bowl

3. **Add BBQ Sauce**
   - Start with 8-10 oz Kansas City BBQ sauce
   - Pour over pulled chicken
   - Reserve remaining BBQ sauce for wrap assembly (2.5 oz per wrap × 6 wraps = 15 oz needed separately)

4. **Mix Thoroughly**
   - Using spoon, spatula, or clean gloved hands, toss chicken and BBQ sauce together
   - Ensure every piece of chicken is coated
   - Mix for 30-60 seconds
   - Scrape sides and bottom of bowl

5. **Assess Coating**
   - Chicken should be well-coated but not swimming in sauce
   - Should look glossy and moist
   - No dry, uncoated pieces
   - Not soupy or dripping excessively
   - If too dry: Add 2-4 oz more BBQ sauce, mix again
   - If too wet: Drain excess sauce or use chicken as-is (excess will drain during assembly)

6. **Taste and Adjust**
   - Sample small amount (using clean spoon)
   - Flavor should be: sweet, tangy, smoky from chicken marinade, with BBQ sauce character
   - If needs more BBQ flavor: Add more sauce
   - If too sweet: Add splash of apple cider vinegar
   - Document any adjustments

7. **Portion**
   - Divide BBQ chicken into 6 portions of approximately 4 oz each
   - Can pre-portion or portion during assembly
   - Keep covered and refrigerated

8. **Store**
   - Cover bowl or transfer to storage container
   - Label: "Pulled BBQ Chicken - Date - Use Today"
   - Refrigerate at 38°F-40°F
   - Use within 2-4 hours for optimal quality (chicken can dry out or become soggy if held too long)

**Quality Standards:**
- Chicken well-coated with BBQ sauce
- Glossy, moist appearance
- Not swimming in excess sauce
- Rich BBQ flavor
- Tender texture

**Training Notes:**
- Teach proper mixing technique (gentle but thorough)
- Show how to assess appropriate sauce level
- Practice taste testing and adjustment
- Discuss timing: mix close to assembly time
- Emphasize importance of temperature control

**Yield:** Approximately 24-28 oz BBQ pulled chicken (ready for assembly)

### 6.4 Component Preparation

**Time Required:** 10 minutes  
**Skill Level:** Basic  
**Training Focus:** Portioning, draining moisture, organization

**Procedure:**

**SHREDDED PEPPERJACK CHEESE:**

1. **Portion Cheese**
   - Measure or weigh: 2 oz per wrap (12 oz total)
   - Pre-shredded cheese: Fluff to separate if clumped
   - Fresh-shredded: Best quality but more time-consuming
   - Place each portion in separate container or section
   - Keep refrigerated

**CREAMY COLESLAW:**

1. **Prepare Coleslaw**
   - If using store-bought: Open container, stir well
   - Check consistency: Should be creamy, not watery
   - If excessively watery: Drain in fine-mesh strainer for 5-10 minutes
   - Press gently to remove excess liquid (don't squeeze dry)

2. **Portion Coleslaw**
   - Measure: 3.5 oz per wrap (21 oz total)
   - Can use scale or 7 Tbsp per wrap
   - Place each portion in separate container or portion during assembly
   - Keep refrigerated

**BREAD & BUTTER PICKLE CHIPS:**

1. **Drain Pickles**
   - Open jar/container
   - Pour pickles into colander or strainer
   - Let drain for 5 minutes
   - Shake gently to remove excess brine
   - Pat with paper towels if very wet
   - **Important:** Excess moisture makes wrap soggy

2. **Portion Pickles**
   - Measure: 3 oz per wrap (18 oz total)
   - Approximately 6-8 pickle chips per wrap (depending on size)
   - Can count out portions or weigh
   - Keep refrigerated in drained state

**BBQ SAUCE FOR WRAP INTERIOR:**

1. **Portion BBQ Sauce**
   - Measure Kansas City BBQ sauce: 2.5 oz per wrap (15 oz total)
   - Can use squeeze bottle for easy application
   - Or use spoon/spreader during assembly
   - Keep at room temperature or refrigerated

**Storage:**
- All components should be refrigerated at 38°F-40°F until assembly
- Pre-portioning saves time during assembly
- Cover all components to prevent drying

**Quality Standards:**
- Pepperjack: Fresh, not dried out
- Coleslaw: Creamy, crunchy, not watery

## 7. ASSEMBLY PROTOCOL

### 7.1 Pre-Assembly Verification

**Before beginning assembly, verify:**
- [ ] All components prepared and cold (≤40°F)
- [ ] Assembly work surface cleaned and sanitized
- [ ] Gloves donned (new pair for assembly)
- [ ] All components portioned and ready
- [ ] Packaging materials ready (deli paper, boxes, labels)
- [ ] Assembly area is free of contaminants

- Pulled BBQ chicken: ≤40°F
- Shredded pepperjack: ≤40°F
- Coleslaw: ≤40°F
- Pickles: ≤40°F

### 7.2 Assembly Procedure (Cold Assembly Only)

**Time Required:** 20 minutes for 6 wraps  
**Skill Level:** Intermediate  
**Training Focus:** Assembly with saucy/moist components, wrap rolling, preventing sogginess

**Step-by-Step Assembly:**

1. **Prepare Assembly Line**
   - Lay out all 6 garlic herb wraps (12") on clean work surface
   - Have all components within easy reach
   - Position in order of layering for efficiency
   - Have BBQ sauce ready in squeeze bottle or with spoon

2. **Apply BBQ Sauce to Wrap**
   - Spread 2.5 oz Kansas City BBQ sauce in center of each wrap
   - Use spoon or squeeze bottle
   - Spread in 6-8 inch circle in center
   - Leave 2-inch clear border on all edges (critical for successful rolling)
   - This layer adds flavor and helps bind ingredients

3. **Layer 1: Pulled BBQ Chicken**
   - Place 4 oz pulled BBQ chicken on BBQ sauce
   - Distribute evenly in center
   - Keep within sauce circle
   - Chicken is main protein component

4. **Layer 2: Shredded Pepperjack Cheese**
   - Sprinkle 2 oz shredded pepperjack over chicken
   - Distribute evenly across chicken
   - Cheese adds heat and creamy texture

5. **Layer 3: Creamy Coleslaw**
   - Place 3.5 oz coleslaw over cheese
   - Spread gently but don't compress
   - Coleslaw adds crunch and cooling contrast
   - **Note:** This is a moist ingredient - assembly soon after adding

6. **Layer 4: Bread & Butter Pickle Chips (Top)**
   - Arrange 3 oz pickle chips on top of coleslaw
   - Distribute evenly (approximately 6-8 chips)
   - Pickles on top prevents them from making bottom soggy
   - Sweet pickles complement BBQ flavor

7. **Assess Fullness**
   - This is a substantial, moist wrap
   - Filling should be generous but not overflowing
   - Should be able to see clear 2-inch border
   - If overfilled (can't roll), reduce portions slightly for remaining wraps

**Rolling Technique:**

8. **Fold Bottom**
   - Fold bottom edge of wrap up and over filling
   - Tuck edge slightly under filling
   - Creates bottom seal
   - **Important:** Press gently to help seal

9. **Fold Sides**
   - Fold left side in toward center (about 2 inches)
   - Fold right side in toward center (about 2 inches)
   - Sides should overlap slightly in center
   - Creates side seals

10. **Roll Forward**
    - Starting from bottom (already folded), roll wrap forward
    - **Roll tightly** to compress ingredients (especially important with moist filling)
    - Use both hands to maintain even pressure
    - Continue rolling until seam side is down
    - **Note:** Tight roll prevents sogginess and holds shape

11. **Assess Quality**
    - Wrap should feel firm and compact
    - Should maintain shape when lifted
    - No tearing of tortilla
    - Filling distributed evenly throughout
    - Some BBQ sauce/moisture may appear at ends (this is normal)

**Packaging:**

12. **Wrap in Deli Paper Immediately**
    - Place rolled wrap in center of deli paper (12" x 12")
    - Wrap tightly in deli paper
    - Deli paper absorbs excess moisture
    - Secure with tape if needed

13. **Place in Box or Clamshell**
    - Insert paper-wrapped sandwich into packaging
    - Ensure snug fit
    - Package protects wrap and contains any moisture

14. **Label Package**
    - Apply pre-printed label
    - Ensure label includes: Product name, ingredients, allergens, date, use-by date
    - Label clearly visible

15. **Refrigerate Immediately**
    - Place packaged wraps in refrigerator
    - Store at 38°F-40°F
    - **CRITICAL:** Refrigerate within 15 minutes of assembly completion
    - This wrap particularly benefits from immediate refrigeration (moist ingredients)

### 7.3 Assembly Quality Checks

**After Each Wrap:**
- [ ] Ingredients distributed evenly
- [ ] Wrap rolled tightly (very important for this wrap)
- [ ] No tears in tortilla
- [ ] Filling not leaking excessively from ends (some moisture normal)
- [ ] Deli paper secure (absorbing moisture)
- [ ] Package neat and professional

**After Full Batch:**
- [ ] All 6 wraps assembled
- [ ] All wraps refrigerated within 15 minutes
- [ ] Work area cleaned
- [ ] Leftover components properly stored or discarded
- [ ] Batch log completed

- Best consumed within 3 days for optimal texture

## 9. FOOD SAFETY & HACCP

### 9.1 Critical Control Points (CCPs)

**CCP 1: Raw Chicken Handling**
- **Hazard:** Bacterial contamination (Salmonella, Campylobacter)
- **Critical Limits:**
  - Store raw chicken at ≤40°F
  - Use separate cutting boards and utensils
  - No cross-contamination with ready-to-eat foods
- **Monitoring:** Temperature checks, visual separation
- **Corrective Action:** Discard contaminated ready-to-eat food; sanitize surfaces immediately
- **Verification:** Document on batch log

**CCP 2: Chicken Cooking Temperature**
- **Hazard:** Pathogenic bacteria survival
- **Critical Limit:** Internal temperature ≥165°F in all pieces
- **Monitoring:** Use calibrated thermometer; check thickest part of each piece
- **Corrective Action:** If below 165°F, continue cooking until reached; discard if cannot reach temperature
- **Verification:** Document temperature on batch log

**CCP 3: Cooked Chicken Cooling**
- **Hazard:** Bacterial growth in danger zone (41°F-135°F)
- **Critical Limit:** Cool from 165°F to ≤40°F within 2 hours
- **Monitoring:** Verify chicken cooled before pulling
- **Corrective Action:** If exceeds 2 hours, evaluate safety; ensure faster cooling next time
- **Verification:** Document temperatures on batch log

**CCP 4: Cold Chain During Assembly**
- **Hazard:** Bacterial growth
- **Critical Limit:** All components ≤40°F; wraps refrigerated within 15 minutes
- **Monitoring:** Check temps before assembly; time process
- **Corrective Action:** Immediately refrigerate if exceeds 15 min; discard if components >40°F
- **Verification:** Temperature log and time stamps

**CCP 5: Storage Temperature**
- **Hazard:** Bacterial growth during storage
- **Critical Limit:** Store at 38°F-40°F
- **Monitoring:** Check refrigerator temperature twice daily
- **Corrective Action:** Move to functioning cooler if >40°F; discard if >2 hours above 40°F
- **Verification:** Refrigerator temperature log

### 9.2 Food Safety Procedures

**Personal Hygiene:**
- Wash hands before production and after handling raw chicken
- Wear clean apron, hairnet, gloves
- Change gloves after raw chicken, before ready-to-eat
- No jewelry, nail polish, artificial nails

**Cross-Contamination Prevention:**
- Use separate cutting boards: raw chicken, cooked chicken
- Use separate utensils (never reuse without sanitizing)
- Sanitize surfaces after raw chicken contact
- Never place cooked product on surface that held raw chicken (unless sanitized)
- Storage: Raw chicken on bottom shelf

**Marinating Safety:**
- Always marinate in refrigerator
- Use food-safe containers
- Label clearly as "Raw Chicken"
- Never reuse marinade that contacted raw chicken
- Discard used marinade

**Time and Temperature Control:**
- Maintain cold chain throughout
- Minimize room temperature time
- Complete assembly within 15 minutes per batch
- Never leave product at room temperature >2 hours

**Allergen Management:**
- Label clearly with all allergens
- Prevent cross-contact
- This wrap contains: Wheat, Milk, Eggs, Soy
- May contain: Mustard, Fish (Worcestershire)

### 9.3 Sanitation Procedures

**Before Production:**
- Clean and sanitize all surfaces and equipment
- Wash hands thoroughly

**During Production:**
- Sanitize after raw chicken handling
- Change gloves frequently
- Clean spills immediately

**After Production:**
- Clean and sanitize all equipment
- Clean surfaces
- Empty trash
- Sweep and mop

### 9.4 Product Dating and Storage

**Labeling:**
- Product name: "BBQ Chicken Wrap"
- Packed date
- Use-by date: Packed + 3 days (conservative) or + 5 days (optimal)
- Storage: "Keep refrigerated 38-40°F"

**Storage:**

## 12. TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE

### 12.1 Common Issues and Solutions

| **Issue** | **Possible Cause** | **Solution** | **Prevention** |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------|----------------|
| **CHICKEN & MARINADE** | | | |
| Chicken dry after cooking | Overcooked; temperature too high | Stop at 165°F; use 275°F; cover tightly | Use thermometer; low-and-slow method |
| Chicken doesn't pull apart | Undercooked; not tender enough | Cook longer (up to 120+ min); check tenderness | Verify very tender before removing |
| Not enough smoky flavor | Insufficient liquid smoke; not marinated long enough | Increase liquid smoke to 1½ Tbsp; marinate 12+ hours | Follow recipe; ensure coating |
| Too much smoky flavor | Excess liquid smoke | Reduce to 2 tsp; balance with more BBQ sauce | Start with less; can always add |
| Marinade too sweet | Too much sugar | Reduce brown sugar to 3 Tbsp | Taste and adjust |
| **ASSEMBLY & QUALITY** | | | |
| Wrap becomes soggy | Excess moisture from coleslaw/pickles | Drain thoroughly; tight rolling; immediate refrigeration | Drain well; deli paper absorbs |
| Wrap tears | Overfilled with moist ingredients | Reduce portions; don't overfill | Leave adequate border |
| BBQ sauce leaks | Too much sauce; not rolled tightly | Reduce sauce; roll tighter | Apply in center only |
| Coleslaw watery | Not drained | Drain in strainer 5-10 min | Check before portioning |

### 12.2 Quality Recovery

**If chicken undercooked:**
- Return to oven, continue cooking
- Verify 165°F before proceeding
- Document

**If wrap tears:**
- Use fresh wrap, reassemble
- Don't serve damaged wrap

**If components compromised:**
- When in doubt, throw out
- Document
